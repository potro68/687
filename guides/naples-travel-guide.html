Naples travel guide: where to stay, what to see and 3 perfect days

The best neighbourhoods to sleep in, the Veiled Christ, the underground city, real Neapolitan pizza and Pompeii as a day trip. Everything for a first visit, including what to expect in winter.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 28 November 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer

Naples is loud, chaotic and one of the most rewarding cities in Italy: a historic centre that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the best pizza in the world, underground cities and, 35 minutes away by train, Pompeii. Three days is the right amount of time: two for the city, one for Pompeii. Sleep in the historic centre or near Via Toledo, walk everywhere, and book the Veiled Christ before you leave home.

Where to stay in Naples

  • Centro Storico (Spaccanapoli, Via dei Tribunali): the best choice for a first visit. You are on foot from the pizzerias, Sansevero, the Duomo and Naples Underground. Streets are lively and noisy: ask for a room facing the courtyard.
  • Via Toledo and Quartieri Spagnoli: central, between the old town and the seafront, with two metro line 1 stations (Toledo and Dante). Very lively in the evening.
  • Chiaia and the seafront (Lungomare): more elegant and quieter, with views of the bay and Castel dell'Ovo. A 15–20 minute walk to the old town.
  • Vomero: residential, calm and up on the hill, reached by funicular. Good for families, less atmosphere in the evening.
  • Near Napoli Centrale (Piazza Garibaldi): handy for early trains and Pompeii, but it is the least pleasant area at night. Fine for one night, not for the whole stay.

Compare hotels in Naples for your dates: in January prices are among the lowest of the year.

What to see

  1. Spaccanapoli and Via dei Tribunali: the two long, straight streets of the Greek city, full of churches, shops and pizzerias. Just walk.
  2. Sansevero Chapel and the Veiled Christ: Giuseppe Sanmartino's marble Christ under a transparent veil is the most famous sculpture in Naples. Booking is mandatory, it is closed on Tuesdays and photos are not allowed inside.
  3. San Gregorio Armeno: the street of nativity-scene workshops, open all year, at its busiest from November to early January.
  4. National Archaeological Museum (MANN): the mosaics, frescoes and statues from Pompeii and Herculaneum. See it before Pompeii and the ruins make much more sense. Closed on Tuesdays.
  5. Naples Underground: Greek-Roman tunnels and cisterns 40 metres below the old town, visited with a guide.
  6. Duomo and the Treasure of San Gennaro, the city's patron saint.
  7. Piazza del Plebiscito, Royal Palace and Galleria Umberto I, then the Lungomare to Castel dell'Ovo at sunset.
  8. Toledo metro station: often called one of the most beautiful in Europe. A metro ticket is all you need.
  9. Vomero and Castel Sant'Elmo: take the funicular up for the best view of the bay and Vesuvius.

Check the closing days. Sansevero and the Archaeological Museum are both closed on Tuesdays: if one of your three days is a Tuesday, make it your Pompeii day.

What to eat

  • Pizza margherita or marinara, soft and with a high, airy crust: the Neapolitan pizza is a UNESCO intangible heritage tradition. Expect queues at the famous names, especially at lunchtime.
  • Pizza fritta and pizza a portafoglio (folded in four, eaten walking) for a few euros.
  • Sfogliatella (riccia or frolla) and babà for breakfast with an espresso at the bar.
  • Cuoppo: a paper cone of fried seafood or fried vegetables and croquettes.
  • Pasta e patate con la provola, genovese and ragù at a trattoria.

Coffee is part of the culture: drink it standing at the counter, and if you see "caffè sospeso" it means someone has paid for a coffee for the next person who can't afford one.

Getting there and around

  • By train: Frecciarossa and Italo reach Napoli Centrale from Rome in about 1h10, from Florence in about 3 hours and from Milan in about 4h30. Compare them in one search on Omio.
  • From the airport: the Alibus links Capodichino airport to Piazza Garibaldi (Napoli Centrale) and the port (Molo Beverello) for €5.
  • Getting around: the old town is best on foot. Metro line 1 (Garibaldi, Duomo, Università, Toledo, Dante, Museo, Vanvitelli) connects almost everything; the funiculars go up to Vomero.
  • To Pompeii: the Circumvesuviana train from Porta Nolana or Garibaldi to Pompei Scavi – Villa dei Misteri, about 35 minutes, then a few steps to the entrance.
  • Don't drive: traffic, parking and the city's restricted traffic zones make a car useless in Naples. Read our ZTL guide if you arrive by car.

Naples in winter (January)

January is one of the best months for Naples if you don't need the beach: few tourists, low hotel prices and days that are often sunny and mild, around 13–14°C, with some rainy days. Until 6 January the city is still dressed for Christmas and the nativity scenes are everywhere. At Pompeii, the winter timetable (November to March) is shorter: 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 15:30, so go in the morning. The ruins are closed on 25 December and 1 January.

Practical tips

  • Pompeii tickets: the standard ticket costs €20 in 2026 (plus €1 if you buy online). Buy it on the official channels, not from resellers at the station.
  • Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket on crowded buses, the Circumvesuviana and in Piazza Garibaldi, as in every big city.
  • Watch the scooters: in the narrow streets of the old town they come from both directions.
  • Many state museums are free on the first Sunday of the month, but expect crowds.
  • For a day on the coast, see our Amalfi Coast without a car guide: ferries leave from Molo Beverello.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How many days do you need in Naples?

Three days is ideal for a first visit: two days for the city and one for Pompeii. With two days, focus on the historic centre, the Veiled Christ and the seafront.

Where is the best area to stay in Naples?

For a first visit, the historic centre or the area around Via Toledo: you can walk to almost everything. Chiaia and the seafront are quieter and more elegant.

How do I get from Naples to Pompeii?

Take the Circumvesuviana train from Porta Nolana or Garibaldi to Pompei Scavi – Villa dei Misteri. It takes about 35 minutes and the entrance is a short walk away.

Do I need to book the Veiled Christ?

Yes. Booking is mandatory at the Sansevero Chapel Museum, which is closed on Tuesdays. Photos are not allowed inside.

Is January a good time to visit Naples?

Yes, if you don't need the beach: there are few tourists, hotels are cheaper and the weather is often mild. Pompeii closes earlier in winter, at 17:00 with last entry at 15:30.
