Osaka in 2 days: the perfect itinerary

Osaka is Japan's kitchen: louder and more relaxed than Tokyo, cheaper than Kyoto. Two days give you the castle, the street food and the neon of Dotonbori — or a full day at Universal Studios.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Day 1 is classic Osaka: Osaka Castle in the morning, Kuromon Market for lunch, Shinsekai and the Tsutenkaku tower in the afternoon and Dotonbori at night. Day 2 is your choice: Universal Studios Japan (a full day, book ahead), or the modern side — Umeda Sky Building, shopping in Shinsaibashi and Amerikamura. Sleep in Namba and you can walk to the food every evening.

Getting to Osaka

  • From Tokyo: the Tokaido Shinkansen reaches Shin-Osaka in about 2h30 by Nozomi; from there the Midosuji subway line goes to Umeda and Namba. Compare options in Tokyo to Kyoto (Osaka is the next stop) or see trains and buses Tokyo → Osaka.
  • From Kyoto: 15 minutes by Shinkansen, or about 30 minutes by JR Special Rapid to Osaka (Umeda) — no seat reservation needed.
  • From Kansai Airport (KIX): the Nankai Rapi:t to Namba (about 40 min) or the JR Haruka to Tennoji and Shin-Osaka. Itami airport (ITM) is for domestic flights.

Day 1: castle, markets and Dotonbori

  1. 9:00 Osaka Castle — walk the park and the moats first (free), then the museum inside the main tower if you like history.
  2. 12:00 Kuromon Market — grilled seafood, wagyu skewers and fruit. Go before 14:00: many stalls close in the afternoon.
  3. 14:30 Shinsekai — retro streets around the Tsutenkaku tower, and the place for kushikatsu (fried skewers; never double-dip in the shared sauce).
  4. 18:00 Dotonbori — the canal, the neon and the Glico running man sign. Eat your way along: takoyaki, okonomiyaki, ramen. Hozenji Yokocho alley is a quiet, lantern-lit break.

Day 2: Universal Studios or modern Osaka

Option A — Universal Studios Japan: a full day. Tickets are dated and sell out at weekends and in holidays; Super Nintendo World often needs a timed-entry ticket or an Express Pass. Book USJ tickets in advance and arrive before opening.

Option B — modern Osaka: the Umeda Sky Building Floating Garden Observatory (sunset is the best time), the cafés of Nakazakicho, then shopping along Shinsaibashi-suji and the street style of Amerikamura. With an extra half day, Nara (deer and Todai-ji) is about 40 minutes from Namba by Kintetsu train.

Getting around

The Midosuji subway line connects Shin-Osaka, Umeda, Shinsaibashi, Namba and Tennoji: it is the backbone of your visit. Pay with an IC card (Suica, PASMO or ICOCA all work). If you plan many sights in one day, the Osaka Amazing Pass includes unlimited subway and bus rides and free entry to many attractions.

Where to stay

Namba / Shinsaibashi is the best for food and nightlife and has direct trains to Kansai Airport. Umeda is better for train connections (JR, Kyoto, Kobe) and day trips. Compare hotels in Osaka.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How many days do you need in Osaka?

Two days are ideal: one for the castle, the markets and Dotonbori, and one for Universal Studios or modern Osaka. Add half a day for Nara.

Is Osaka better than Kyoto as a base?

Osaka has cheaper hotels, more nightlife and direct trains to Kansai Airport; Kyoto is only 15–30 minutes away by train, so many travellers sleep in Osaka and visit Kyoto.

How do I get from Kansai Airport to Osaka?

The Nankai Rapi:t reaches Namba in about 40 minutes; the JR Haruka goes to Tennoji and Shin-Osaka.

Do I need to book Universal Studios Japan in advance?

Yes: tickets are dated and sell out at weekends and in holidays, and Super Nintendo World often needs timed entry.
