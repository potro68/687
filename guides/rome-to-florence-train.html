Rome to Florence by train: the complete guide

High-speed trains do it in about an hour and a half, from the centre of Rome to the centre of Florence. Here is which train to take, how to pay less and the mistakes to avoid.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Take a high-speed train, either Trenitalia's Frecciarossa or Italo: about 1 hour 30 minutes from Roma Termini to Firenze Santa Maria Novella, with departures roughly every 15–30 minutes from early morning to late evening. Booked a few weeks ahead, fares often start around €15–20; bought on the day they are usually €45–60 or more. Compare Frecciarossa and Italo times and fares in one search.

Frecciarossa or Italo? On this route they are practically equal in speed and comfort. Read Trenitalia vs Italo for the differences in fares, changes and lounges.

Stations: where you leave and arrive

  • Roma Termini is the main station, on metro lines A and B. Some trains, especially Italo, also stop at Roma Tiburtina: check which one is on your ticket, they are several kilometres apart.
  • Firenze Santa Maria Novella (SMN) is in the centre: the Duomo is about 10 minutes on foot.
  • A few trains arrive at Firenze Campo di Marte instead, on the east side: not ideal if your hotel is near SMN.
  • Arriving at Fiumicino airport? See Fiumicino to Rome: some high-speed trains to Florence also leave directly from the airport.

Tickets and prices

  1. Buy on the official Trenitalia or Italo websites and apps, or on a comparison site such as Omio that shows both. Avoid unknown sites selling "official tickets" at a markup.
  2. The cheapest fares (Economy, Super Economy on Trenitalia; Low Cost on Italo) have limited or no changes and refunds. Pay a little more if your plans may change.
  3. High-speed tickets have a seat number and are valid only on that train: no validation needed. A digital ticket on your phone is fine.
  4. Fridays, Sundays and holiday weekends sell out first: book those early.
Strike day? Trains can be cancelled during national rail strikes; guaranteed services still run. See Train strikes in Italy before you travel.

Regional trains: cheap but slow

Regionale trains take around 4 hours, often with a change, but have a fixed low fare and no reservation. A paper regional ticket must be validated before boarding, or you risk a fine: see How to validate a train ticket in Italy. Digital tickets bought in the app are already tied to a date and time.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Going to Termini when your train leaves from Tiburtina, or getting off at Campo di Marte instead of SMN.
  • Arriving late: high-speed trains open the platform about 10–15 minutes before departure and do not wait. The departure board shows the platform (binario).
  • Leaving luggage unattended at Termini: keep bags close on the platform and on board. Big suitcases go in the racks at the end of the carriage.
  • Driving: a car is useless in central Florence and the ZTL fines are expensive. Rent one only for Chianti or the Tuscan hills.

Tips

  • A day trip from Rome to Florence is possible with an early train, but two days are better: see Florence in 2 days.
  • Book the Uffizi and the Accademia (David) before you go: see skip-the-line tickets.
  • Continuing to Venice? The same trains continue north: see Rome, Florence and Venice in 7 days.
  • Want to stay near the station? Compare hotels near Santa Maria Novella.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How long is the train from Rome to Florence?

About 1 hour 30 minutes on Frecciarossa or Italo high-speed trains from Roma Termini to Firenze Santa Maria Novella. Regional trains take about 4 hours.

How much is the train from Rome to Florence?

High-speed fares often start around €15–20 if booked a few weeks ahead and are usually €45–60 or more when bought on the day. Regional trains are cheaper but much slower.

Should I book the Rome to Florence train in advance?

Yes for high-speed trains: prices rise as the date gets closer and Fridays, Sundays and holidays sell out. Regional trains need no reservation.

Which station in Florence should I arrive at?

Firenze Santa Maria Novella (SMN), in the centre, about 10 minutes on foot from the Duomo. Check your ticket: a few trains arrive at Firenze Campo di Marte.
