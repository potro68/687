Christmas in Italy: the best markets and where to go

From Alpine wooden huts with mulled wine to the nativity workshops of Naples and the Epiphany fair in Rome: how Italians really celebrate, where to go and what is closed on the big days.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 17 November 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

For the classic "snow, wooden huts and mulled wine" Christmas, go to South Tyrol and Trentino: Bolzano, Merano, Bressanone and Trento have the most famous markets in Italy, all reachable by train. For something more Italian and less Alpine, see the nativity workshops of Naples and the Piazza Navona fair in Rome. Plan around 8 December (when the season starts), and remember that on 25 December and 1 January almost everything is closed.

The Alpine markets: South Tyrol and Trentino

Bolzano

The oldest and biggest Christmas market in Italy, in Piazza Walther under the cathedral. In 2026 it opens on the evening of 26 November and the stalls are open 27 November – 6 January, 10:00–19:00 (24 December until 16:00, closed on 25 December). Expect around 112 huts, concerts almost every evening and very big crowds on Saturdays. The station is two minutes away on foot.

Merano

Smaller and more elegant, along the river promenade, with the spa town feel. Combine it with an afternoon at the Terme Merano thermal baths: outdoor pools with mountain views in winter are the real treat. About 40 minutes by train from Bolzano.

Bressanone (Brixen)

The most intimate of the three, around the cathedral square, with about 44 huts: half a day is enough. Perfect as a stop between Bolzano and the ski areas.

Trento

Opens a week earlier (around 20 November), spread across Piazza Fiera and Piazza Cesare Battisti. More city than mountain, with good food stalls and the Dolomites around.

Smart move: sleep in one town (Bolzano is the most central) and visit the others by regional train. Weekdays are far calmer than Saturdays and the 8 December long weekend.

Naples, Rome and the south

  • Naples – Via San Gregorio Armeno: the street of nativity-scene (presepe) craftsmen, open all year but at its peak in December. Tiny shepherds, pizzerias in miniature and, every year, figurines of footballers and politicians. Go on a weekday morning: in December weekends it is shoulder to shoulder.
  • Rome – Piazza Navona: the Christmas and Befana fair fills the piazza until 6 January, with sweets, toys and a carousel. Add the giant tree and nativity scene in St Peter's Square.
  • Midnight Mass at St Peter's on 24 December: tickets are free but must be requested in advance from the Vatican; you can still watch from the square on big screens.

Other places worth it

  • Gubbio (Umbria): the "world's largest Christmas tree", made of lights on the slope of Monte Ingino, lit around 7 December.
  • Verona: markets in Piazza dei Signori and a giant illuminated star coming out of the Arena.
  • Montepulciano (Tuscany): a Christmas village in a hilltop medieval town, easy to combine with Tuscany wine country.
  • Milan: the "Oh Bej! Oh Bej!" traditional market around 7 December (Sant'Ambrogio) and the tree in Piazza del Duomo.

Before you book a day for a big museum, check its holiday hours: the Vatican Museums, for example, are closed on 25 and 26 December. Trains run, but with reduced timetables on the holidays: book high-speed trains early, Christmas weeks sell out.

What to eat

  • Panettone (Milan) and pandoro (Verona): buy them from a real bakery, not the supermarket.
  • In the Alpine markets: vin brulé (mulled wine), strudel, canederli (bread dumplings) and speck.
  • Naples: struffoli (honey fried dough balls) and the Christmas Eve fish dinner.
  • Rome: Christmas Eve is fish too; on the 25th, pasta in broth and roasts.

Tips

  • Book hotels in Bolzano and Merano now for December weekends: compare hotels in Bolzano.
  • Go by train: a car in the old towns means ZTL zones and full car parks. See ZTL in Italy.
  • Bring layers: the Alps are often below zero after sunset.
  • Check for train strikes before you travel, even in December.
  • Combining with Venice? See Venice in 2 days, often foggy and magical in winter.

FAQ

Quick answers.

When do Christmas markets in Italy open in 2026?

The big South Tyrol markets (Bolzano, Merano, Bressanone) open on 26 November 2026 and run until 6 January 2027; Trento usually starts about a week earlier.

What is the best Christmas market in Italy?

Bolzano is the oldest and largest. Merano is more elegant and has thermal baths, Bressanone is smaller and more intimate.

Is everything closed in Italy on Christmas Day?

Most museums, shops and many restaurants are closed on 25 December and 1 January. Trains run on a reduced holiday timetable.

Is Italy worth visiting at Christmas?

Yes: fewer tourists in the art cities, lights and nativity scenes everywhere, and the Alpine markets. Book hotels early for December weekends.
