Safety alerts in Japan: what tourists should know

Japan warns early and well — if you know where to look. Here is what the main alerts mean and what to do as a visitor.

Updated 14 November 2026 · 6 min read · By the 687 team
IN THIS GUIDEShort answerThe app to installTyphoons and heavy rainHeatTsunami and earthquakesJ-AlertTips

The short answer

Before you leave, install Safety tips, the official multilingual app of the Japan Tourism Agency: it sends alerts for earthquakes, tsunami, weather and heat in English and other languages. Your foreign phone may not receive Japanese emergency messages, so don't count on it. When an alert arrives, follow local instructions: in Japan they are clear and quick.

The app to install

  • Safety tips (Japan Tourism Agency): free, 15 languages, push notifications for earthquake early warnings, tsunami warnings, weather warnings and heatstroke alerts.
  • JMA website (Japan Meteorological Agency): warnings and risk maps, also in English.
  • Many phones bought outside Japan don't receive the national emergency alerts: the app fills that gap.

Typhoons and heavy rain

Typhoon season runs from summer to early autumn, and the rainy season (tsuyu) from about June to July. Heavy rain can cause flooding and landslides quickly.

  • In 2026 Japan renamed its weather warnings and linked them to 5 alert levels: the higher the number, the more serious. From Level 4 you should move to a safe place if local authorities tell you to.
  • Trains, ferries and flights are often stopped before a typhoon arrives: on those days check your connections early and keep a flexible plan.
  • Stay away from rivers, the coast and steep slopes during heavy rain.

Heat and heatstroke alerts

Japanese summers are hot and humid. The Heat Stroke Alert is issued by region when the risk of heat illness is very high, based on the WBGT index (temperature plus humidity and sun).

  • On alert days, do outdoor sightseeing early in the morning or after sunset.
  • Drink often, rest in air-conditioned places and wear a hat.
  • Dizziness, headache, nausea or confusion are warning signs: stop, cool down and get help if they get worse.

Tsunami and earthquakes

An earthquake early warning gives you a few seconds before the shaking: protect your head and don't run outside. If you are on the coast and a tsunami warning is issued, go immediately to high ground or to the upper floors of a solid building, following the blue tsunami evacuation signs. Read our full guide: What to do in an earthquake in Japan.

J-Alert

J-Alert is the national system that broadcasts emergency messages through loudspeakers, TV and phones, for example in case of a missile launch or a major disaster. If you hear it, go inside a solid building or underground and follow the instructions.

Tips

  • Save your hotel address in Japanese on your phone: it helps with taxis and with staff in an emergency.
  • Emergency numbers: 110 police, 119 fire and ambulance.
  • Travel insurance that covers medical costs is strongly recommended in Japan.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Which app should tourists use for alerts in Japan?

Safety tips, the free official app of the Japan Tourism Agency, in 15 languages, with alerts for earthquakes, tsunami, weather and heat.

Will my foreign phone receive Japanese emergency alerts?

Not always: many phones bought abroad do not receive them. Install Safety tips to be sure.

When is typhoon season in Japan?

Mostly from summer to early autumn. Trains and flights can stop before the typhoon arrives.

What does a Heat Stroke Alert mean?

That the risk of heat illness is very high in that region. Avoid outdoor activity in the hottest hours and drink often.
