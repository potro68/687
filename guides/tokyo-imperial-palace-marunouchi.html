Tokyo Imperial Palace & Marunouchi: a free half day next to Tokyo Station

Castle walls, moats, a Japanese garden, the red-brick station and a rooftop over the Shinkansen: a calm, free half day in the very centre of Tokyo, with opening hours, closed days and a simple route.

The short answer

Right next to Tokyo Station there is half a day of Tokyo that costs nothing: the stone walls and moats of the old Edo Castle, the Imperial Palace East Gardens, the famous Nijubashi bridge and, ten minutes away, the red-brick station and the glass towers of Marunouchi. It is the calmest corner of central Tokyo, perfect for your first morning after a long flight or for the day you leave on the Shinkansen.

The Imperial Palace East Gardens (free)

The East Gardens (Kōkyo Higashi Gyoen) are the part of the palace you can walk into on your own: no ticket and no booking. Inside are the huge stone base of the old castle keep (Tenshudai), the old guardhouses, lawns where the shogun's inner residence once stood and the Ninomaru garden, a classic Japanese garden with a pond, at its best with autumn leaves in late November and early December.

Closed every Monday and Friday, from 28 December to 3 January and on some event days. Public holidays are open (except the Emperor's Birthday); if a Monday holiday is open, the Tuesday after is closed. Check the official calendar before you go.

There are three gates: Ōtemon (about 15 minutes on foot from the Marunouchi North Exit of Tokyo Station, 5 minutes from Otemachi Station), and Hirakawamon and Kitahanebashimon (5 minutes from Takebashi Station). Enter at Ōtemon and leave from Kitahanebashimon if you want to continue to the Kitanomaru park and the Budokan.

The inner-grounds tour (free, with registration)

The palace buildings where the Imperial Family lives and works are not open, but the Imperial Household Agency runs a free guided walk of part of the inner grounds, passing close to the palace and the iron bridge behind Nijubashi. You can register online in advance on the official site, or try for a same-day place at the Kikyōmon Gate in the morning. In cherry-blossom and autumn seasons the online places go fast.

Nijubashi and the outer garden

The wide gravel plaza of the Kōkyo Gaien (outer garden), with its pine trees, is always open. From here you get the classic photo of Nijubashi: the stone bridge over the moat with the white Fushimi turret behind it. Walk along the moat towards Hibiya or Otemachi: the reflections of the office towers in the water, with the old walls and autumn trees in front, are one of the best free views of modern Tokyo. The 5 km path around the palace is also Tokyo's favourite jogging loop, run anticlockwise.

Marunouchi and Tokyo Station

Between the palace and the station lies Marunouchi, Tokyo's business district, wide and calm at weekends. Don't miss:

  • Tokyo Station Marunouchi building: the restored red-brick station from 1914. Look up inside the domes of the north and south halls.
  • KITTE Garden: the free rooftop on the 6th floor of the KITTE building, right in front of the station. You look down on the station roof and the Shinkansen coming and going; it is beautiful at sunset and at night.
  • Naka-dori: the tree-lined street with shops and cafés. From early November to mid-February it is lit up for the Marunouchi Illumination in the evening.
  • The Shin-Marunouchi building, whose 7th-floor terrace also looks over the station.

A 4-hour route

  1. 9:00 Tokyo Station, Marunouchi side: photos of the red-brick facade.
  2. 9:20 Walk west along the wide avenue to the moat and the Kōkyo Gaien: Nijubashi.
  3. 10:00 Along the moat to Ōtemon and into the East Gardens: Tenshudai, Ninomaru garden (1–1.5 hours).
  4. 11:45 Back to Marunouchi: lunch in the basements of the office towers, where set lunches are cheaper than you'd expect.
  5. 13:00 KITTE Garden for the view of the trains, then Naka-dori. If you leave on the Shinkansen, the station is one minute away.
On a Monday or Friday? The East Gardens are closed, but the outer garden, Nijubashi, the moat walk, Marunouchi and KITTE Garden are not. Swap the gardens for a longer walk along the moat to Hibiya Park.

Practical tips

  • Go in the morning: in winter the East Gardens close at 16:00 and last entry is 15:30.
  • There are almost no shops inside the gardens: bring water and buy snacks at a konbini before you go in.
  • Big suitcases are a problem on the gravel: leave them in the lockers at Tokyo Station.
  • Respect the area: no picnics on the lawns where it is not allowed, no drones, and keep your voice low near the palace. More in our Japanese etiquette guide.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Is the Tokyo Imperial Palace free to visit?

Yes. The East Gardens and the outer garden with the Nijubashi view are free and need no ticket. The guided tour of the inner grounds is free too, but you must register online or on the day at Kikyomon Gate.

When are the Imperial Palace East Gardens closed?

Every Monday and Friday, from 28 December to 3 January and on some event days. Public holidays are open except the Emperor's Birthday; if a Monday holiday is open, the following Tuesday is closed.

What time do the East Gardens open?

From 9:00. Closing changes with the season: 16:00 from November to February (last entry 15:30), up to 18:00 from mid-April to August.

How do I walk from Tokyo Station to the Imperial Palace?

Take the Marunouchi exits and walk west along the wide avenue: the moat is about 10 minutes away and Otemon Gate of the East Gardens about 15 minutes.

Is KITTE Garden free?

Yes. KITTE Garden is a free rooftop on the 6th floor of the KITTE building in front of Tokyo Station, with views over the station and the Shinkansen.
