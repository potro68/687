Mount Fuji day trip from Tokyo

Kawaguchiko is the easiest place to see Mount Fuji from Tokyo: two hours by bus or train, a lake, a pagoda and — with a little luck — the mountain in full view.

The short answer

Go to Kawaguchiko, the lake town at the foot of the mountain. The highway bus from Busta Shinjuku is the cheapest (about 1h45, around ¥2,200); the Fuji Excursion train from Shinjuku is the most comfortable (about 1h50, around ¥4,130). Leave early in the morning: Mount Fuji is most often visible before clouds build up.

The JR Pass covers only the JR part of the Fuji Excursion: the last section on the Fujikyu line is paid separately. No time to organise? A Mount Fuji day tour from Tokyo handles transport and stops for you.

A simple day at Kawaguchiko

  • 7:00–8:00 — leave Shinjuku by bus or train.
  • Morning — the north shore of Lake Kawaguchi, the classic view of Fuji over the water.
  • Late morning — Chureito Pagoda (about 400 steps), the famous postcard view of the pagoda in front of the mountain.
  • Lunch — hoto, the local thick noodle soup.
  • Afternoon — the panoramic ropeway or a walk by the lake, then back to Tokyo. The last buses leave in the evening, the train earlier.

Will you actually see Mount Fuji?

Not always: the mountain often hides in clouds, especially in summer. The clearest months are usually from late autumn to winter, and mornings are better than afternoons. Check the live cameras and the weather the evening before, and keep a flexible date if you can.

Day trip or one night?

If Fuji is a highlight of your trip, sleep one night at the lake: you get the sunrise and a second chance if the first day is cloudy. Many ryokan have onsen with a view of the mountain — compare hotels in Kawaguchiko.

Tips

  • Book the bus or the reserved train seats a few days ahead for weekends and holidays.
  • Going on to Kyoto? You can continue the next day: see Tokyo to Kyoto and the 7-day Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka itinerary.
  • Bring warm layers: by the lake it is much colder than in Tokyo.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How do I get from Tokyo to Mount Fuji?

Take the highway bus from Busta Shinjuku (about 1h45, around ¥2,200) or the Fuji Excursion train from Shinjuku (about 1h50, around ¥4,130) to Kawaguchiko.

Is the Fuji Excursion covered by the JR Pass?

Only the JR section. The Fujikyu line section to Kawaguchiko is paid separately.

When is Mount Fuji most visible?

Usually from late autumn to winter, and in the morning rather than the afternoon.

Is one day enough for Mount Fuji?

Yes for Kawaguchiko and Chureito Pagoda. Sleep one night if you want sunrise and a second chance with the weather.
