JAPAN TRAVEL GUIDE · TRAINS

Shinkansen guide for first-time visitors

Japan’s bullet trains are fast, punctual and easy to use — once you know which station to go to, which train to take and what to do with your suitcase.

Updated 25 October 2026 · 7 min read · By the 687 team

1. Use the right station

The most common first-timer mistake: in several cities the Shinkansen does not stop at the main city-center station.

3. Nozomi, Hikari or Kodama?

On the busiest route (Tokyo – Kyoto – Osaka – Hiroshima – Hakata) there are three main types of train:

  • Nozomi — the fastest, with the fewest stops.
  • Hikari — slightly slower, a few more stops.
  • Kodama — stops at every station; useful for smaller towns, slow for long trips.
JR Pass holders: the standard Japan Rail Pass covers Hikari and Kodama. To ride Nozomi or Mizuho you need an additional special ticket for pass holders. Check the current price before you buy the pass.

4. Reserved or non-reserved seats

  • Non-reserved cars (自由席, jiyūseki) are cheaper and first come, first served. Fine outside rush hours.
  • Reserved seats (指定席, shiteiseki) guarantee a seat. Book them for weekends, holidays and peak seasons such as cherry blossom, Golden Week, Obon and New Year.
  • Green Car is first class: wider seats and quieter cars.

On the platform, signs on the floor show where each car number stops. Wait in line there — trains are on time and stops are short.

5. Big luggage rules

On the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen, luggage is measured by length + width + height:

Total sizeWhat to do
Up to 160 cmNo special reservation needed. Overhead racks fit most carry-on cases.
160 – 250 cmReserve a seat with an oversized baggage area when you book. Without it you may be charged a fee (¥1,000) and directed where to store the bag.
Over 250 cmNot allowed on board as normal luggage.

Travelling light makes everything easier. Luggage forwarding (takkyūbin) from hotel to hotel is cheap, reliable and offered at most hotel front desks.

6. How to buy tickets

  • At the station: ticket machines (with English) or JR ticket offices.
  • Online: the smartEX service for Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu lines, and JR East’s online booking for lines from Tokyo to the north and to Kanazawa.
  • Paper tickets: insert them into the gate and take them back — you need them again to exit at your destination.

As a reference, a reserved one-way seat from Tokyo to Kyoto costs roughly ¥14,000. Prices vary by train and season, so check the official booking site.

7. Tips for the ride

  • Mount Fuji: on the Tokaido Shinkansen from Tokyo towards Kyoto, Fuji is on the right side — choose a window seat E in standard cars. You need clear weather.
  • Buy an ekiben (station lunch box) before boarding; eating on board is normal.
  • Keep phone calls for the space between cars, and set your phone to silent.
  • Arrive on the platform a few minutes early — doors close exactly on time.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Which station does the Shinkansen use in Osaka?

Shin-Osaka. The Shinkansen does not stop at Osaka Station; Shin-Osaka is one stop away on the JR Kyoto Line, about 4 minutes.

Can I ride the Nozomi with a JR Pass?

Not with the standard pass alone. JR Pass holders need an additional special ticket to ride Nozomi and Mizuho trains. Hikari and Kodama are covered.

Do I need to reserve a Shinkansen seat?

Not always. Non-reserved cars are fine outside peak times, but reserve for weekends, holidays and busy seasons, and whenever you travel with large luggage.

What are the Shinkansen luggage rules?

On the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu lines, bags over 160 cm (length + width + height) up to 250 cm need a seat with an oversized baggage area reserved in advance. Bags over 250 cm are not allowed.

How long is the Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto?

About 2 hours 15 minutes on the fastest Nozomi trains. Hikari trains take a little longer.
