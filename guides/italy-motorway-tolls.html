Italian motorway tolls: how the autostrada works for tourists

Tickets, the right lane at the booth, what it really costs, Telepass for visitors and what to do if you can't pay — the toll road explained before your first casello.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 26 November 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

Most Italian motorways (autostrade, green signs, letter A + number) are toll roads. You take a ticket when you enter and pay when you exit, by card or cash. Stay out of the yellow Telepass lanes unless your car has the device, keep the ticket safe, and expect roughly €7–8 every 100 km in a normal car.

How a toll booth works

  1. At the entrance (casello) stop at a lane with a ticket symbol and press the red button: a ticket comes out and the barrier opens.
  2. Keep the ticket somewhere safe, not on the dashboard in the sun. If you lose it you may be charged as if you had driven from the farthest entrance.
  3. At the exit insert the ticket, pay the amount on the screen and the barrier lifts. The toll is calculated on the distance you actually drove.

A few stretches work differently: some city bypasses and short sections have a fixed price paid at a barrier on the road, and some newer sections are "free flow" — no booths at all, cameras read the plate and you pay online afterwards. Signs before the section tell you which system it uses.

Which lane to choose at the exit

Sign above the laneWho can use itFor tourists

Entered a yellow lane by mistake? Do not reverse. Press the help button (assistenza) or drive through: you'll get a report to pay the toll afterwards. Reversing at a booth is dangerous and fined.

How much it costs

The price depends on the distance and on the operator of each stretch (Italy has several). As a rough guide for a normal car:

Exact prices change every year: check your route on the Autostrade per l'Italia journey calculator before you leave. Fuel at motorway service stations usually costs more than in town: fill up before you join the autostrada if you can.

Telepass for tourists: worth it?

Telepass is the electronic box that lets Italians drive through the yellow lanes without stopping. For a short holiday you don't need it: paying by card at the booth works fine. It can make sense for a long road trip with many toll days.

  • Rental cars: some companies offer a Telepass device for a daily fee. Ask at the desk and make sure you know whether it is active before you use a yellow lane.
  • Pay-per-use offers: Telepass and other providers have plans where you pay only on the days you use the motorway, but most require an Italian bank account or app registration.
  • Sicily: not every provider covers all Sicilian motorways — check before you rely on it.

If you can't pay at the booth

Card refused, no cash, lost ticket? It happens. Press the help button and you'll receive a "Rapporto di mancato pagamento" (unpaid toll report) with the amount due.

  • You have 15 days to pay without extra charges — online on the motorway operator's website, in the Telepass app, or at service points.
  • After 15 days, collection costs are added and the bill can reach your rental company, which will charge you again with an admin fee.
  • On "free flow" sections without booths the same 15-day rule applies: pay online using your plate number.

On the road: Tutor, rest stops and emergencies

  • Tutor / average speed cameras: on many motorways the system measures your average speed between two points. Braking at the camera doesn't help.
  • Rest stops (aree di servizio) every 30–50 km have fuel, toilets and a bar: an espresso and a panino at the counter are part of the Italian road trip.
  • Breakdown: stop in the emergency lane, put on the reflective vest before you get out and place the warning triangle. The orange SOS columns along the road call for help, or dial 803.116 for ACI roadside assistance (112 for emergencies).
  • Headlights must be on, also during the day.

Prices and rules are indicative and can change — always check before you travel.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How do motorway tolls work in Italy?

On most autostrade you take a ticket at the entrance booth and pay at the exit, by card or cash. The amount depends on the distance driven.

How much are tolls in Italy?

As a rough guide, about €7–8 per 100 km for a normal car. Rome to Florence costs around €20.

Can tourists use the Telepass lanes?

Only if the car has a working Telepass or compatible device. Without it, use the white (cash/all) or blue (cards) lanes.

What if I can't pay the toll in Italy?

You receive an unpaid toll report and have 15 days to pay online without extra charges. After that, collection costs are added.
