Suica and PASMO in Japan: how IC cards work for tourists

One tap for trains, metro, buses and convenience stores. Which card to get, how to top it up and the mistakes that cost tourists time at the gates.

Updated 9 November 2026 · 6 min read · By the 687 team
IN THIS GUIDEShort answerWhich cardOn your phoneWhere it worksTop upWhat it doesn't coverTips

The short answer

Suica and PASMO are rechargeable IC cards you tap at the ticket gate instead of buying a ticket for every ride. They work the same way on almost all trains, subways and buses in Tokyo and in Japan's big cities, and you can pay with them in convenience stores and many vending machines.

Suica and PASMO are interchangeable for travelers: same gates, same shops. Pick whichever you can get first.

Which card to get

OptionBest forGood to know
Welcome SuicaShort-stay visitorsSold at airports and some major stations for tourists. No deposit, but valid for a limited period and the remaining balance is not refunded — top up little by little.
Regular Suica / PASMOLonger trips or repeat visitsSmall refundable deposit; balance can be refunded when you return the card.
Suica on iPhone (Apple Wallet)iPhone usersNo physical card, top up with your bank card in Wallet, works with Express Mode — no need to unlock the phone.

Suica on your phone

  1. Open Wallet on iPhone and tap +.
  2. Choose Transit Card → Suica (or PASMO).
  3. Add a starting amount with your card.
  4. At the gate, hold the top of the phone near the reader.

Many Android phones sold outside Japan cannot add Suica — in that case use a physical card.

Where it works

  • JR trains, private railways, subways and most city buses in Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto, Nagoya, Fukuoka, Sapporo and other areas.
  • Convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart), many vending machines, lockers and some taxis and restaurants.
  • Look for the IC logo on the reader.

How to top up

Use the charge machines at stations (choose English, insert the card, pay cash) or pay at a convenience store counter. Many machines accept only cash — keep some yen with you. On iPhone you top up directly in Wallet.

What the card does not cover

  • Shinkansen reserved seats: you need a proper Shinkansen ticket (or JR Pass reservation). Read the Shinkansen guide.
  • Crossing between some regions on one journey can fail at the gate — if it does, go to the staffed window and pay the difference.
  • Limited express surcharges may need a separate ticket.

Tips that save time

  • Keep at least ¥1,000–2,000 on the card so a gate never stops you.
  • Tap in and tap out — always.
  • One card per person, even for children (children's fares need a child card).
  • Lost a physical card? Unregistered cards cannot be blocked, so don't load large amounts.

FAQ

Quick answers.

What is the difference between Suica and PASMO?

For tourists there is practically none: both are rechargeable IC cards accepted on the same trains, subways, buses and shops.

Can I use Suica on the Shinkansen?

Not for reserved Shinkansen seats. You need a Shinkansen ticket or a JR Pass reservation; Suica covers local trains, subways and buses.

Can I add Suica to my iPhone?

Yes. In Apple Wallet tap + and choose Transit Card, then Suica or PASMO, and top up with your bank card.

Can I get a refund on Welcome Suica?

No. Welcome Suica has no deposit, but any remaining balance is not refunded, so top up only what you need.
