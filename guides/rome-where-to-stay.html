Where to stay in Rome: the best areas, explained

In Rome the area you sleep in decides how much you walk, how much you spend and how the city feels at night. Here are the neighbourhoods that work best for a first visit, and who each one is really for.

By Gabriel P. · Updated 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

For a first visit of 2–4 nights, sleep in the Centro Storico (around Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Campo de' Fiori) or in Monti, next to the Colosseum: you will walk almost everywhere and come back to the hotel in the afternoon. If you care most about evenings and food, choose Trastevere. If you want quiet and good value near the Vatican, choose Prati. Pick Termini only if you arrive by train early or leave late, and only on the right streets.

Centro Storico: everything on foot

The historic centre is the Rome of postcards: Pantheon, Piazza Navona, Trevi Fountain, Campo de' Fiori, all within a 15-minute walk. It's the best choice for a short stay because you lose no time on transport. The downsides: hotels cost more, rooms are often small in old buildings, and some streets are noisy until late. There is no metro in the heart of it, but you won't need it.

Best for: first-timers, couples, 2–3 night stays. Compare hotels in the centre.

Monti: the Colosseum on your doorstep

Between Via Nazionale, Via Cavour and the Colosseum, Monti is a village inside the city: ivy-covered streets, small wine bars, vintage shops and trattorias full of Romans. You can walk to the Colosseum and the Forum in minutes, and the Cavour and Colosseo metro stops (line B) connect you to Termini. Slightly cheaper than the centre and with a real neighbourhood feel.

Best for: those who want character without being in the busiest streets.

Trastevere: the most atmospheric

Across the Tiber, Trastevere is cobblestones, ochre houses, lanterns and dinners outside (the photo at the top is here). It's the most romantic area to come back to at night and it's full of restaurants and bars. What to know: it's loud on weekend nights, there's no metro (tram 8 and buses), and the Colosseum is a 30–40 minute walk. For quieter nights, look at the streets towards Viale di Trastevere or up near the Gianicolo.

Best for: food lovers, couples, second visits. See places to stay in Trastevere.

Prati and the Vatican: quiet and practical

North of the Vatican, Prati is elegant, flat and calm: wide streets, good shopping on Via Cola di Rienzo, honest restaurants and metro line A (Ottaviano, Lepanto). You are steps from St Peter's and the Vatican Museums, so you can enter at opening time, and 20–25 minutes on foot from Piazza Navona. Rooms are usually bigger and better value than in the centre.

Best for: families, longer stays, light sleepers. If you plan the Vatican early in the morning, read our Colosseum and Vatican tickets guide.

Termini and Esquilino: convenient, but choose well

The area around Roma Termini station has the most hotels and the lowest prices, plus both metro lines, the airport trains and buses. It's ideal for an early train or the Leonardo Express to Fiumicino. However, the streets south of the station (Esquilino, Piazza Vittorio side) can feel rough at night. Prefer the north-west side, towards Via Nazionale, Piazza della Repubblica and Castro Pretorio, and always read recent reviews about the street.

Best for: one night before a departure, tight budgets.

Other areas worth knowing

  • Spanish Steps / Via del Corso (Tridente): luxury shopping and elegant hotels, central and safe, the most expensive area.
  • Testaccio: real Roman food market and trattorias, few tourists, metro B (Piramide). Good for a second trip.
  • San Giovanni: residential, cheaper, metro A and C, 15–20 minutes on foot from the Colosseum.
  • Avoid as a base: hotels near the ring road (GRA) or by the airport, unless you have a car: you'll spend hours commuting.

Booking tips

  • The city tax (tassa di soggiorno) is charged per person per night and usually paid at the hotel, often in cash: it is not always included in the online price.
  • Many hotels are on upper floors of old palazzi: check that there is a lift and air conditioning (essential from June to September).
  • In the centre a car is a problem: most of it is a ZTL (restricted traffic zone). Read our ZTL guide before you drive in.
  • Arriving at Fiumicino? See how to get from the airport to Rome and choose a hotel near your arrival point.
  • Book early for Easter, spring and the Christmas holidays, the most expensive periods.

FAQ

Quick answers.

What is the best area to stay in Rome for first-timers?

The Centro Storico (Pantheon, Navona, Campo de' Fiori) or Monti, next to the Colosseum: from both you can walk to almost all the main sights.

Is it safe to stay near Termini station?

Generally yes, but choose the north-west side towards Via Nazionale and Piazza della Repubblica. Streets south of the station can feel unpleasant at night.

Is Trastevere a good area to stay?

Yes, for atmosphere and dinners. It is noisy on weekend nights and has no metro, so pick a quieter street if you sleep lightly.

Is the city tax included in the hotel price in Rome?

Not always. It is charged per person per night and is often paid directly at the hotel.
