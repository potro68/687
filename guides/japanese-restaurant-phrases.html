JAPAN TRAVEL GUIDE · FOOD & LANGUAGE

Japanese restaurant phrases every tourist needs

From the door to the bill: the short, polite phrases that get you through any meal in Japan, written in Japanese, romaji and English.

Updated 8 November 2026 · 5 min read · By the 687 team
IN THIS GUIDEEnteringThe menuOrderingAllergiesPayingEtiquette

Entering the restaurant

Staff will greet you with いらっしゃいませ (irasshaimase, “welcome”). You don’t need to answer it — a nod is enough. Then show how many people you are:

二人です
Futari desu
Two people.
一人です
Hitori desu
Just one person.
予約しています
Yoyaku shite imasu
I have a reservation.
禁煙席はありますか？
Kin’en-seki wa arimasu ka?
Do you have a non-smoking table?

Counting people: hitori (1), futari (2), san-nin (3), yo-nin (4), go-nin (5). Showing the number with your fingers also works perfectly.

Asking for the menu

英語のメニューはありますか？
Eigo no menyū wa arimasu ka?
Do you have an English menu?
写真付きのメニューはありますか？
Shashin-tsuki no menyū wa arimasu ka?
Is there a menu with pictures?
おすすめは何ですか？
Osusume wa nan desu ka?
What do you recommend?
これは何ですか？
Kore wa nan desu ka?
What is this?

For the full English-menu phrase, pronunciation and the answers you may hear, see our guide to asking for an English menu in Japanese.

Ordering food and drinks

これをください
Kore o kudasai
This one, please (pointing).
これを二つください
Kore o futatsu kudasai
Two of these, please.
お水をください
O-mizu o kudasai
Water, please.
生ビールをください
Nama bīru o kudasai
A draft beer, please.

Objects are counted differently from people: hitotsu (1), futatsu (2), mittsu (3), yottsu (4). Water and green tea are usually free and refilled without asking.

Allergies and dietary needs

Japanese menus rarely list allergens in English, so it is worth saying it clearly or showing it on your phone:

アレルギーがあります
Arerugī ga arimasu
I have an allergy.
えびアレルギーです
Ebi arerugī desu
I’m allergic to shrimp.
肉は食べられません
Niku wa taberaremasen
I can’t eat meat.
これに卵は入っていますか？
Kore ni tamago wa haitte imasu ka?
Does this contain egg?
Replace ebi (shrimp) with your allergen: kani (crab), tamago (egg), komugi (wheat), gyūnyū (milk), pīnattsu (peanut), soba (buckwheat). Many broths contain fish (dashi), so vegetarians should ask: 魚のだしは入っていますか？ (Sakana no dashi wa haitte imasu ka?).

Paying the bill

お会計お願いします
O-kaikei onegaishimasu
The bill, please.
カードで払えますか？
Kādo de haraemasu ka?
Can I pay by card?
別々でお願いします
Betsubetsu de onegaishimasu
Separate bills, please.
ごちそうさまでした
Gochisōsama deshita
Thank you for the meal.

In most restaurants you pay at the register near the exit, not at the table: take the bill slip with you. Crossing your index fingers in an “X” also signals that you want the bill.

Four etiquette rules worth knowing

  • No tipping. It is not expected and can cause confusion. Good service is simply included.
  • Slurping noodles is fine. In ramen and soba shops it is normal, not rude.
  • Don’t stick chopsticks upright in rice or pass food chopstick-to-chopstick: both are linked to funerals.
  • Otōshi: in izakaya, a small starter you didn’t order is a normal table charge.

If you only learn two words, make them sumimasen (“excuse me”, to call staff) and kore o kudasai (“this one, please”). With pointing and a smile, they will get you through almost any meal.

FAQ

Quick answers.

What are the most useful Japanese phrases for restaurants?

“Sumimasen” to call staff, “Kore o kudasai” to order by pointing, “Eigo no menyū wa arimasu ka?” for an English menu and “O-kaikei onegaishimasu” for the bill.

How do I say I have a food allergy in Japanese?

Say “Arerugī ga arimasu” (I have an allergy) and name it, for example “Ebi arerugī desu” for shrimp. Showing it written on your phone is the safest option.

Do you tip in restaurants in Japan?

No. Tipping is not part of Japanese restaurant culture; paying the bill is enough.

How do I ask for the bill in Japan?

Say “O-kaikei onegaishimasu”, then usually pay at the register near the exit.
