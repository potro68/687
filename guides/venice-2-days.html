Venice in 2 days: the perfect itinerary

Two days are enough to see the best of Venice without rushing: the classics on day one, the lagoon islands and the quiet side of the city on day two.

The short answer

Day 1 is for the classics: the Grand Canal by water bus, St Mark's Square, the Basilica, the Doge's Palace and the Rialto Bridge, with cicchetti in Cannaregio in the evening. Day 2 is for the lagoon: Murano and Burano in the morning, then Dorsoduro and a sunset on the Zattere. Sleep at least one night in Venice: you see the city without the crowds and you don't pay the access fee.

How to get to Venice

By train you arrive at Venezia Santa Lucia, right on the Grand Canal: about 2h25 from Milan, 2h05 from Florence and 4 hours from Rome on high-speed trains. Get off at Santa Lucia, not at Venezia Mestre (that is the mainland station). You can compare train times and prices on Omio.

By plane you land at Marco Polo (VCE): buses reach Piazzale Roma in about 25 minutes, the Alilaguna water bus takes about an hour but drops you closer to many hotels. Low-cost flights also use Treviso, about an hour away by bus.

Coming by car? Cars stop at Piazzale Roma or Tronchetto, where parking is expensive. A car only makes sense if you continue to the Dolomites, the Prosecco hills or Lake Garda: in that case it is easier to pick it up at Venice airport when you leave.

Day 1: St Mark's, Rialto and the Grand Canal

  • Morning: from the station take vaporetto line 1 along the whole Grand Canal to San Marco: it is the cheapest "tour" of Venice.
  • St Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace: the queues in high season are long, so book the Doge's Palace ticket online and choose a morning slot. From inside the palace you cross the Bridge of Sighs.
  • Afternoon: walk through the alleys to the Rialto Bridge. The Rialto market is open in the morning (closed on Sundays).
  • Evening: move to Cannaregio for cicchetti and a spritz in the bacari along the Fondamenta della Misericordia.

Day 2: Murano, Burano and Dorsoduro

  • Early morning: from Fondamente Nove take the vaporetto to Murano to see the glass furnaces, then continue to Burano, the island of colourful houses. Leave early: by lunch the boats are full.
  • Afternoon: back in the city, explore Dorsoduro: the Accademia Gallery, the Peggy Guggenheim Collection (closed on Tuesdays) and the church of Santa Maria della Salute.
  • Sunset: walk along the Zattere, facing the Giudecca, the most relaxed sunset in Venice.

Where to sleep in Venice

AreaGood for
San MarcoEverything on foot, but the most expensive and crowded

Mestre (mainland)Much cheaper, 10 minutes by train or tram, still exempt from the access fee

Prices in the historic centre rise a lot at weekends and during Carnival: compare hotels in Venice or look at Mestre if your budget is tight.

Getting around

Venice is walked: the historic centre is small, but the alleys are a maze, so keep offline maps on your phone. The vaporetto (ACTV water bus) is expensive as a single ticket: if you ride it more than two or three times, a 24h or 48h pass is better value. A traghetto gondola crossing of the Grand Canal costs just a few euros and is a nice alternative to a full gondola ride.

Tips

  • Visiting on a peak day without sleeping in Venice? Check the access fee first.
  • Combining Venice with other cities? See Rome, Florence and Venice in 7 days and Trenitalia vs Italo.
  • In autumn and winter check the acqua alta (high tide) forecast in the morning: St Mark's Square floods first.

Times and prices are indicative and can change — always check before you travel.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How many days do you need in Venice?

Two days are enough to see the main sights and the islands of Murano and Burano without rushing. One day is possible, but only for the classics around St Mark's and Rialto.

Is it better to sleep in Venice or Mestre?

Venice is more atmospheric and you see it without crowds in the evening; Mestre is much cheaper and 10 minutes away by train. Guests in both are exempt from the access fee.

Which train station is closest to Venice?

Venezia Santa Lucia, right on the Grand Canal. Venezia Mestre is on the mainland.

Do I need to book the Doge's Palace in advance?

In high season it is strongly recommended: online tickets with a time slot let you skip most of the queue.
