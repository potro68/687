Last Supper in Milan: how to get tickets in time

Only 40 people every 15 minutes, tickets released three months ahead and gone in hours. When to book, the official prices, what to do if it's sold out and the masterpiece most visitors miss, behind them.

The short answer

Leonardo's Last Supper is on the wall of the old refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan, and you can only see it with a booked time slot: groups of up to 40 people, for 15 minutes, Tuesday to Sunday. Tickets are released in batches about three months ahead and the best dates go in hours. If you know when you're going to Milan, booking the Last Supper should be one of the very first things you do.

When tickets go on sale

Reservations open every three months, always at noon on the official platform:

Planning spring 2027? The February–April batch opens in December. Check the exact day on the official site and set an alarm: popular weekends and Easter dates disappear first.

Two more chances: extra tickets are released online every Wednesday at noon for the following week, and the free tickets for the first Sunday of the month go on sale online on the Wednesday before, at noon, and run out in minutes.

How to book

  • Online on the official Vivaticket platform linked from cenacolovinciano.org: up to 5 tickets, and a maximum of 5 tickets per person per calendar year.
  • Call centre +39 02 92800360: up to 9 tickets.
  • Prices: €15 full (25 and over), €2 for ages 18–25, free under 18, but everyone needs a reservation, children included.
  • Tickets are named: write the names exactly as on your ID, because they can't be changed later.
Beware of resellers. Many sites sell "skip the line" Last Supper tickets at three or four times the price. The only official place for the €15 ticket is the platform linked from the museum's site.

Sold out? Your options

  1. Check again every Wednesday at noon, when extra tickets for the following week are released.
  2. Call the call centre +39 02 92800360. Our tip from experience: when the website shows nothing, phoning often works, and when we booked, new places came out on Friday at noon. The official site says online extras come out on Wednesdays, so try both days.
  3. Look for cancellations: refresh the official calendar in the days before your visit, especially early in the morning.
  4. Guided tours: tour operators have their own allocation, so they often have places when the official site shows none. They cost much more, but include a guide who explains the painting.
  5. Be flexible: weekday mornings and the last slots of the day are the easiest to find.

On the day

  • Be at the ticket office next to the entrance at least 30 minutes before your time, with your payment receipt: if you are late, you lose the ticket.
  • Opening hours: Tuesday to Sunday, 8:15–19:00 (last entry 18:45). Closed on Mondays, 1 January and 25 December.
  • You go in through a couple of glass "air-lock" rooms that keep the air clean, then you are in the refectory.
  • Photos are allowed, without flash. But you only have 15 minutes: take a couple of pictures, then put the phone away and look.

What you'll see in 15 minutes

The Last Supper (1494–1498) fills the end wall: Jesus has just said that one of the apostles will betray him, and the twelve react in groups of three. Leonardo did not paint in true fresco but in tempera on dry plaster, which is why it began to flake while he was still alive and why it looks so delicate today. Stand back towards the middle of the room: the painted ceiling and windows continue the real room, as if the table were part of the refectory.

Then turn around. Most visitors forget that on the opposite wall there is another huge painting: the Crucifixion by Giovanni Donato da Montorfano, finished in 1495 while Leonardo was working in front of it. At the sides, now barely visible, are Ludovico il Moro, Duke of Milan, and his wife Beatrice d'Este, probably added by Leonardo himself.

Around Santa Maria delle Grazie

Arrive early and visit the church of Santa Maria delle Grazie itself (free), with its Renaissance tribune by Bramante. Corso Magenta, the street outside, has elegant cafés and pastry shops for a coffee before your slot. From here the Duomo is about 20 minutes on foot or a few stops on metro line M1 from Conciliazione or Cadorna. For the rest of the city, see our Milan in 2 days itinerary.

FAQ

Quick answers.

How far in advance should I book the Last Supper?

As early as you can. Tickets go on sale every three months for the following three months, and popular dates sell out within hours of the release.

How much does a Last Supper ticket cost?

€15 full price for adults 25 and over, €2 for ages 18–25, free under 18. Every ticket, including free ones, must be reserved.

Can I take photos of the Last Supper?

Yes, photos are allowed without flash. You have 15 minutes in the room, so take a few and then enjoy the painting.

What if the Last Supper is sold out?

Check the official site every Wednesday at noon for extra tickets, watch for cancellations, or book a guided tour, which has its own allocation.

Is the Last Supper open on Mondays?

No. It is open Tuesday to Sunday from 8:15 to 19:00 (last entry 18:45) and closed on Mondays, 1 January and 25 December.
