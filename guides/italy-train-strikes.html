Train strike in Italy: what travelers should do

Train strikes (scioperi) are common in Italy, but they are announced in advance and some trains always run. With a little planning, a strike day is a delay — not a disaster.

How train strikes work in Italy

  • They are announced in advance, usually several days before.
  • National rail strikes often run from 21:00 one day to 21:00 the next. Regional or company strikes can be shorter, for example a morning or a daytime window.
  • A strike can involve Trenitalia, Italo, regional operators such as Trenord, or only some of them. Read which companies are involved.

Where to check strike dates

  • The Italian Ministry of Transport’s official strike calendar (scioperi.mit.gov.it).
  • The news pages and apps of Trenitalia, Italo and regional operators such as Trenord.
  • Your booking emails: operators often contact passengers whose trains may be affected.
Check a few days before every train day, and again the evening before. Strike dates sometimes change or are called off.

Guaranteed trains and time bands

Italian rules require some essential services to run during a strike:

ServiceWhat runs
Regional trains, weekdaysGuaranteed in the bands 06:00 – 09:00 and 18:00 – 21:00.
Long-distance trains (Frecce, Intercity, Italo)Trains on the guaranteed list published by each operator for that strike.
Trains already travelingUsually reach their destination if they are due to arrive within about an hour of the strike start.

A train that is not on the guaranteed list is not automatically cancelled — many still run — but you cannot count on it.

Refunds and changes

Trenitalia

  • Frecce and Intercity: you can ask for a refund up to the scheduled departure of your train, or change to another train subject to availability.
  • Regional tickets: refund requests usually must be made by midnight before the strike day.

Italo

If your train is not guaranteed, you can change to a guaranteed train or cancel free of charge before departure, online or through Italo’s customer service.

Rules can be updated for each strike, so always read the operator’s notice for your date.

On the strike day

  1. Travel inside the guaranteed bands if you depend on regional trains — leave early in the morning or in the evening.
  2. Move to a guaranteed long-distance train if your own train is not on the list.
  3. Consider alternatives: long-distance buses, a domestic flight between distant cities, or a rental car for a road day.
  4. Keep the day flexible. Put museums and walks first and the transfer at a time you know is covered.
  5. Watch the departure boards. Cancelled trains show soppresso; delays show ritardo.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Do trains run during a strike in Italy?

Some do. Regional trains are guaranteed on weekdays from 06:00 to 09:00 and 18:00 to 21:00, and Trenitalia and Italo publish lists of guaranteed long-distance trains for each strike.

How long do train strikes in Italy last?

National rail strikes often last 24 hours, typically from 21:00 one day to 21:00 the next. Regional or company strikes can be shorter.

Can I get a refund if my train is affected by a strike?

Yes. Trenitalia allows refunds or changes for Frecce and Intercity up to departure, and for regional tickets usually by midnight before the strike. Italo lets you change to a guaranteed train or cancel free before departure.

Where can I check upcoming train strikes in Italy?

Check the Ministry of Transport’s official strike calendar and the news pages of Trenitalia, Italo and regional operators such as Trenord.

What does sciopero mean?

Sciopero is the Italian word for strike. On departure boards, soppresso means the train is cancelled.
