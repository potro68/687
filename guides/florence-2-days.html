Florence in 2 days: the perfect itinerary

Florence is small and walkable: two days are enough for the Duomo, the Uffizi, Michelangelo's David and a sunset over the Arno — if you book the right things.

The short answer

Day 1 is the historic centre: the Duomo and Brunelleschi's dome, Piazza della Signoria, the Uffizi and the Ponte Vecchio. Day 2 is Michelangelo's David at the Accademia, the San Lorenzo market, then the Oltrarno (Palazzo Pitti and the Boboli Gardens) and sunset at Piazzale Michelangelo. Avoid planning museums on a Monday: the Uffizi, the Accademia and Palazzo Pitti are closed.

How to get to Florence

By train you arrive at Firenze Santa Maria Novella, a 10-minute walk from the Duomo: about 1h35 from Rome, 2h05 from Venice and 1h55 from Milan on high-speed trains. You can compare train times and prices on Omio or read Trenitalia vs Italo.

By plane, Florence airport (FLR, Peretola) is linked to the station by the T2 tram in about 20 minutes. Many international flights land in Pisa: from there it is about an hour to Florence by train.

Driving? The whole historic centre is a ZTL (limited traffic zone) with cameras: leave the car outside the centre or, better, only rent one on the day you leave for Tuscany's hills.

Day 1: Duomo, Uffizi and Ponte Vecchio

  • Morning: start at the Duomo square. Entering the cathedral is free, but the dome climb (463 steps), the Giotto bell tower and the Baptistery need a ticket — and the dome has fixed time slots, so book it.
  • Late morning: walk down Via dei Calzaiuoli to Piazza della Signoria and Palazzo Vecchio.
  • Afternoon: the Uffizi Gallery (Botticelli, Leonardo, Caravaggio). Count at least 2–3 hours and book a timed ticket.
  • Sunset and evening: cross the Ponte Vecchio and have dinner in the Santo Spirito area, the Oltrarno's liveliest square.

Day 2: the David, San Lorenzo and the Oltrarno

  • Early morning: the Accademia Gallery for Michelangelo's David — take the first slot of the day, when queues are shortest.
  • Lunch: the Mercato Centrale in San Lorenzo: food stalls upstairs, lampredotto and Tuscan classics.
  • Afternoon: Palazzo Pitti and the Boboli Gardens, or the Bardini Garden for the view without the crowds.
  • Sunset: walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo (or to San Miniato al Monte, just above) for the classic view of the city.

State museums are often free on the first Sunday of the month, but queues are very long that day. Buy only from the official sites or from well-known resellers.

Where to sleep in Florence

Santa Maria Novella is the most practical (station, day trips to Pisa and Siena). Santa Croce and the Duomo area put you in the middle of everything, while the Oltrarno is quieter, with more local restaurants. Florence charges a city tourist tax per night, paid at the hotel. FAQ

Quick answers.

How many days do you need in Florence?

Two days are enough for the Duomo, the Uffizi, the David and the Oltrarno. With a third day you can add a day trip to Pisa, Siena or Chianti.

Which day are museums closed in Florence?

Monday: the Uffizi, the Accademia and Palazzo Pitti are closed. Plan the Duomo, walks and markets for Monday instead.

Do I need to book the Duomo dome?

Yes. The climb has fixed time slots and sells out days ahead in high season; entering the cathedral itself is free.

Is Florence airport close to the centre?

Yes: the T2 tram links Florence airport (FLR) to Santa Maria Novella station in about 20 minutes.
